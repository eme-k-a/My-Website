## From PLAN §6

### 6. Continuous improvement loop
1. **Capture:** `npm run review` on a deterministic build: screenshots at every stop and dialog on 4 viewports, plus perf stats, axe and Lighthouse.
2. **Score:** R11 rates each stop 1 to 5 on six dimensions, citing screenshots and comparing with the previous round and the approved G2 and G4 reference stills. It also records pass/fail for each acceptance criterion.
3. **File findings:** each goes in `review/findings.json` with a severity (P0 broken, P1 visibly wrong or over budget, P2 polish, P3 idea). Each finding gets a brief, `docs/wp/F-###.md`, scoped to the owner globs of the affected path.
4. **Fix:** at most one fixer per owner area, 5 at once. Each fix is a `fix(F-###)` commit plus a test or a justified rebaseline. R11 re-verifies in the next round.
5. **Stop** after 2 consecutive rounds with no new P0 or P1, at most 2 new P2, averages ≥4.0 and every gate green. Cap: 3 rounds per milestone. Leftovers go to Emeka's backlog. A finding reopened twice is a taste call for Emeka.
6. **After launch:** one round for every content change, plus a monthly round.
7. **Backlog candidates:** ambient sound, a cookieless analytics counter, a custom domain, GLB models (only with `CREDITS.json` and a CC0/OFL license test).

## From EXPERIENCE §1

### 1. Concept

**The exhibition is called "Commit History".** It is a small, dark, contemporary gallery that shows Chukwuemeka (Emeka) Nduka-Nwagbo's work the way `git log` shows commits: newest first.

**The walk:**
1. **Lobby.** The visitor arrives in a lobby. The DOM hero (name, current role, degree, Resume PDF, Email, Classic view) is readable before any 3D loads. Through an arch, the first exhibit's light pool already glows on the right.
2. **Main aisle (work only, newest first).** Nasdaq Verafin, Mysa, Wailo, CFIA. Each work role is one sculptural object in a lit glass case on a plinth. Every step forward is a step back in time.
3. **Optional side wings.** Two doors off the aisle lead to small rooms: Projects (KlarUp, Personal Finance Tracker) and Volunteer and Leadership (Safe Sip, Software Developers of St. John's). Visitors step inside on purpose; scrolling past skips them.
4. **Foundations room.** At the end of the aisle: the degree (Memorial University, B.Eng Computer Engineering, expected May 2027) and three certifications. It is on the main path, never optional, because recruiters must always pass degree status.
5. **Front desk.** The exit: Email, LinkedIn, "Take home the catalogue" (the resume PDF) and GitHub. The end wall reads "You've reached the beginning."

**How time shows in the space:**
- Brass year strips are set into the floor (2026 at the arch, then 2025 down to 2022).
- Spotlights and placard paper grow warmer the further back the work is.
- The current role carries an "ON VIEW NOW" pill.

**Principles (every work package follows these):**
1. **The DOM is the content; WebGL is the stage.** Every readable word lives in HTML: per-stop cards, the story dialog, the hero. 3D text exists only for big labels that duplicate DOM text.
2. **One object per case.** Each exhibit is a single sculptural object built from code primitives with at most one slow motion. Restraint reads as craft; busyness reads as programmer art.
3. **Native scroll, never hijacked.** The page scrolls like a page. The camera follows the scroll position.
4. **A recruiter gets everything in 10 seconds.** Name, current role, degree, Resume PDF, Email and Classic view are in the first phone viewport, with or without JavaScript.
5. **Truth over flourish.** No invented metrics, no logos, no imagery that overstates the work (Verafin shows reporting, never fraud detection).
6. **Computed, not hand-placed.** The layout, numbering, year strips, light warmth and durations are all computed from the content file, so a new job slides everything down the aisle with no hand-edited coordinates.

#### 7.5 Lighting recipe (fixed per tier at boot)
- **Ambient:** hemisphere light, sky `#1A1C22`, ground `#0B0B0D`, intensity 0.15.
- **Real spotlights:** 3 on high, 1 on medium and low (§6.8). Angle 0.38 rad, penumbra 0.6, decay 2, `castShadow` false. Starting intensity 40 cd, tuned by R2 in WP-11 and frozen as a token.
- **Zero shadow maps.**
- **Baked light pools:** `lightPools.ts` paints a CanvasTexture lightmap for each floor zone. Per target: a radial pool of radius 1.6 m, centered 0.3 m from the plinth toward the aisle center, peak alpha 0.85 in its temperature color, smoothstep falloff. Door thresholds get a smaller pool (radius 1.2 m, alpha 0.35, `light-0`) on the aisle floor so doors invite.
- **Contact shadows, rendered once:** painted into the same lightmap (plinth footprint, soft edge 0.25 m, darkness 0.55), plus the plinth-top contact shadow (§5.2).
- **Wall wash:** a shared 256 px "scallop" texture on wall bays behind each target, tinted by its temperature; panel seams every 2 m are baked into it.
- **Environment:** drei `Environment` with `frames={1}`, resolution 256, built from Lightformers: a ceiling strip (`light-0`, intensity 2, 0.3 x 60 m) along the centerline at y 4.9, two side panels (`light-1`, intensity 0.6), and one lobby panel. No HDRI files.
- **Fog:** `FogExp2`, `#0B0B0D`, density 0.045. The next case (12 m) is clearly visible; the one after (24 m) is a faint pool.
- **Tone mapping:** ACES Filmic, exposure 1.0, until WP-11. In WP-11 R2 captures the lobby, verafin and cfia stops under ACES Filmic and AgX side by side, picks one, and freezes it as the token `render.toneMapping` (`'aces' | 'agx'`, with `render.exposure`) in `src/design/tokens.ts`. Output color space sRGB.
- **Vignette:** CSS only, a fixed `pointer-events: none` layer over the canvas: `radial-gradient(ellipse at center, transparent 55%, rgba(11,11,13,0.55) 100%)`. No post-processing package.
- **Tiers:** high = DPR max 2, `MeshReflectorMaterial` floor, 3 spots, beam cones. Medium = DPR max 1.5, env-mapped floor, 1 spot, beam cones. Low = DPR 1, env-mapped floor, 1 spot, no beam cones. Only DPR adapts at runtime.

## From EXPERIENCE §11

### 11. Delights and do-nots

#### 11.1 Six cheap delights
1. **The exhibition itself is a joke for engineers:** "Commit History", newest first, and the end wall that reads "You've reached the beginning."
2. **Museum-label wit on every card:** "Medium" is the tech stack, "Dimensions" is the computed duration, "ON VIEW NOW" marks the current role.
3. **Admission ticket:** "Begin the walk" is styled as a ticket stub with a perforated edge (CSS only). After hydration it is stamped with today's date in the visitor's locale ("15 OCT 2026"; the stamp area has a fixed width in the prerender, so no layout shift). On click the stub tears off (400 ms) and the walk begins. Reduced motion: no tear.
4. **Gift shop at the exit:** the resume PDF is "Take home the catalogue", the LinkedIn link is a postcard on the desk, the email is a sealed envelope, and GitHub is a ledger. Each 3D desk object has a matching DOM button on the desk card.
5. **Light that answers you:** each case's real light fades up as you arrive, and on desktop the lobby portrait warms from duotone to full color over 600 ms on hover (fine pointer only; reduced motion: no change).
6. **"Copy link"** in every story copies its `/exhibit/<id>/` address, which unfurls with that exhibit's own preview card.

#### 11.2 Do not
- **Movement:** no free roam, WASD or pointer lock; no head bob; no autoplaying camera tours; no drag-to-rotate; no custom gestures.
- **Input:** no wheel or touch listeners, no `touch-action: none`, no scroll hijacking, no custom keys beyond Enter and Esc.
- **Routing:** no hash routes, no URL writes on stop arrival, no history entry per Newer or Older.
- **Sound:** none in v1: no ambience, no clicks, no footsteps, no autoplay.
- **Effects:** no post-processing or bloom, no particle swarms, no glitch, typewriter, cursor trails or confetti, no flashing.
- **Rendering:** no transmission glass, no real-time shadows or shadow maps, no runtime tier switches that change light count or material types, no GLB models in v1, no HDRI downloads.
- **Exhibits:** more than one motion, more than 20k triangles, materials outside the library, lights inside exhibits, realistic people, avatars or mannequins.
- **Content:** no company or brand logos (in 3D, icons or images), no invented metrics, no text that exists only in WebGL, no small 3D text, no detection imagery for Verafin, no phone number anywhere on the site, no em dashes.
- **UI:** no floor-plan modal, no 3-height bottom sheet, no swipe-to-close, no hover-only information, no loading screen the visitor cannot read past, no hidden route to the classic resume (it is always one click away).
- **Network:** no requests to other origins: fonts, troika fallbacks and everything else are self-hosted, and detect-gpu is not used.

### 7. Review rubric

R11 scores every stop on six dimensions, D1 to D6, from 1 to 5 (half points allowed). Anchors are written for 1, 3 and 5; 2 and 4 sit between them.

| Dim | Name | What it judges | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Gallery mood | Light, darkness, warmth, calm | Flat or evenly lit room, grey or crushed blacks, cold or clashing light, glare, flicker or noise; reads as a 3D demo, not a gallery | Dark room with a warm pool at the case and the next pool visible, but some areas too bright or too dead (washed walls, clipped pools, fog hiding the next case); calm, not yet inviting | Deep ink room; the case sits in its own warm pool, the next pool glows ahead and the one after is a faint promise; brass and glass catch light without glare; the teal glow is the only cool note; calm, quiet, expensive |
| D2 | Composition and framing | Case placement, card clearance, sightline to the next pool | Case cut off, covered by the card or HUD, or far from its framing point; no sightline ahead; walls or ceiling dominate the frame | Case near its framing point (62% of the width on desktop, 36% of the height on phone) and clear of the card, but cramped, the wall title collides, or the next pool falls out of frame | Case on its framing point within 3%, card and HUD never overlap it, the wall title reads above it, and the next pool sits in the sightline; every stop composes like a photograph |
| D3 | Legibility | DOM card and dialog text, contrast, size; 3D labels at least 14 CSS px | Any text under 4.5:1 or under the type scale, text lost over a bright pool, a 3D label under 14 CSS px, or content that exists only in WebGL | All text meets 4.5:1 and the type scale and 3D labels are at least 14 CSS px, but long lines, awkward wraps or busy backdrops make reading effortful | Card, dialog and labels read at a glance on both viewports; hierarchy follows §3; focus rings are obvious; 3D labels are crisp and repeat the DOM |
| D4 | Story clarity | Does the object say what the work was, without overstating it | The object says nothing about the work, or overstates it (detection imagery for Verafin, logos, invented numbers, copied product UI) | The idea reads once the card has been read; one element is ambiguous or merely decorative | The object tells its story at a glance and the card confirms it; nothing claims more than the work; "The object" text matches what is on screen |
| D5 | Craft consistency | Shared materials, proportions, motion restraint across builders; reads as the work of an engineer | Materials outside the library, mismatched scale or edge radii, more than one motion, fast or jittery animation, z-fighting or floating parts; looks like four people built it | Library materials and slot bounds respected; proportions and motion mostly consistent; one exhibit noticeably busier, glossier or faster than its neighbours | Same material vocabulary, edge radii, scale and one slow eased motion everywhere; joins are precise and nothing floats; the set reads as one engineer's careful work |
| D6 | Object read | "Reads as one object in a 320 px grayscale thumbnail" | The 320 px grayscale thumbnail shows a cluster of parts or noise, with no single silhouette | One main silhouette is recognizable, but secondary parts compete with it or it merges with the plinth or wall | One clear silhouette with strong value contrast against case and wall; the §4 "reads as one object" line can be checked from the thumbnail alone |

**Scoring protocol.**
1. **Capture** on a deterministic build (`?test=1`): desktop at `tier=high` and Pixel 7 at `tier=medium`, every stop and every dialog (`npm run review`, or the `@capture` spec as in WP-19). D6 uses a 320 px wide grayscale copy of the desktop stop shot (`sharp(file).resize(320).grayscale()`).
2. **Score** each stop on D1 to D6 for desktop and for Pixel 7. Every score carries a one-line reason and cites the screenshot path it is based on (for example `review/runs/2026-11-02-g2/verafin-desktop.png`). A score without a cited path does not count.
3. **Compare** with the previous round's scorecard and with the approved reference stills in §10; say what moved and why. Praise or blame is always relative to the anchors above, never absolute.
4. **Average** per stop, per dimension and overall. G2 needs an overall average of at least 3.5 with no open P0 or P1 (WP-19); the improvement loop stops after 2 consecutive rounds with no new P0 or P1, at most 2 new P2 and averages of at least 4.0 (PLAN §6).
5. **File findings** with a severity (P0 broken or inaccessible, P1 visibly wrong or over budget, P2 polish, P3 idea) and the repo path most likely responsible.
6. **At most 3 rounds per milestone** (PLAN §6). Leftovers go to Emeka's backlog; a finding reopened twice is a taste call for Emeka.

### 8. Concept checklists

**Process.**
1. The builder WP captures the exhibit's stop screenshot (desktop at `tier=high`, plus Pixel 7 at `tier=medium`), its reduced-motion frames, and the 320 px grayscale thumbnail of the desktop shot (`sharp(shot).resize(320).grayscale()`), under `out/wpNN/` (never committed), and asks R0 for the R2 review.
2. R2 checks every box from the screenshot, the thumbnail, `__MUSEUM__.stats()` (triangles, `inBounds`), the code (library materials only, one motion, no lights, no text) and the pixel comparison of reduced-motion and `?test=1` frames.
3. **Every box must pass.** A passing box becomes `- [x]`. If any box fails, the Verdict is FAIL with one line per failing box; the builder fixes it and R2 reviews again.
4. The first box of every checklist is the "reads as one object" line; it is judged on the 320 px grayscale thumbnail (D6).

The builder WP's acceptance needs `grep -c '^- \[ \]' review/concepts/<id>.md` to print 0 and the Verdict to read PASS.
6. **Verafin copy guard:** nothing in 3D or in copy may suggest fraud detection or AML models: no red or amber bars, no highlighted or flagged bar, no alert icon, no magnifying glass, no particles or streams.
